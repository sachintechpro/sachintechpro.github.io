Five questions worth asking.

Tap a question for a little context. These are educational prompts, not personalized recommendations.

What should my family know without having access to everything today?

Let family know where to find essential records and whom to call, without sharing passwords or sensitive information unnecessarily. Secure access arrangements can be discussed with an attorney.

How would immediate expenses and responsibilities be handled?

Consider which bills, care needs and other obligations could arise soon after a death or incapacity. Identify who would have legal authority and where available resources might come from.

Are beneficiary designations and estate documents consistent?

Beneficiary designations, property ownership and estate documents may operate differently. Review them together with a qualified attorney so they do not unintentionally conflict.

Who would have the legal authority to act?

A family relationship alone may not grant authority to manage an account or transfer property. Ask an estate attorney about executors, trustees, agents and how each role begins.

What conversation do I need to have with loved ones now?

Begin with values and wishes before discussing numbers: who to contact, which responsibilities matter, and where documents are held. Invite questions and allow loved ones time to understand their roles.

Keep in mind

No legal or insurance contract is created here. Specific arrangements require review with appropriately licensed or qualified professionals.

Don't leave out the essentials.

As life changes, revisit who can act for you and how your wishes will be carried out.
