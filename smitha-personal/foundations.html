Bring these to a conversation

Five questions worth asking.

Tap a question for a little context. These are educational prompts, not personalized recommendations.

What is my most urgent financial decision?

Choose one concrete decision that needs attention, such as organizing bills or reviewing an upcoming expense. Write down the deadline and information you need before comparing any products.

What financial obligations could not wait during an emergency?

Identify housing, utilities, food, caregiving and other obligations that cannot easily be deferred. An emergency plan should also explain who could help if you were unavailable.

Which existing benefits and protections do I already have?

Make a simple inventory of employer benefits, accounts and existing protections without entering identifying numbers here. Review terms and coverage dates to avoid assuming an old arrangement is still active.

How do my short-term needs differ from long-term goals?

Separate near-term liquidity and obligations from goals years away. The time available and consequences of a setback can change which questions should be considered first.

Who should I consult before taking a major legal or financial action?

Estate documents belong with qualified legal counsel, tax questions with a tax professional, and investments or insurance with appropriately licensed professionals. Verify registration, services, fees and conflicts before acting.
