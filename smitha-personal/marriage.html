Bring these to a conversation

Five questions worth asking.

Tap a question for a little context. These are educational prompts, not personalized recommendations.

What money responsibilities will we share, and which will remain separate?

Discuss what each person already owns or owes, which bills will be shared, and how savings decisions will be made. A simple household budget can reveal questions before any major financial commitment.

How would we handle an unexpected loss of income?

Review emergency savings, workplace leave, existing protections, and obligations that continue when income stops. Agree who could manage essential payments and where information is safely kept.

Are our beneficiary and estate planning arrangements consistent with our wishes?

Marriage can change priorities without automatically updating every beneficiary designation or property title. Review existing documents and ask the appropriate professional which legal changes you may need.

What are our near-term and long-term family goals?

Separate goals by timeframe: near-term household stability, a possible home or child, and longer-term retirement or caregiving. Decide what you can realistically prepare for first.

What professional guidance would be helpful before a big decision?

Consider an attorney for property or estate agreements, a tax professional for filing implications, and an appropriately licensed financial or insurance professional for relevant services. Confirm each person's scope and compensation.

Keep in mind

Legal property rules and tax consequences vary. Discuss specific marital-property questions with a qualified California attorney or tax professional.

Don't leave out the essentials.

As life changes, revisit who can act for you and how your wishes will be carried out.
