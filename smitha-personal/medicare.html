Four things to understand

Start with clarity.

Many people first qualify around age 65, but work coverage and special circumstances change the answer. Verify timing with official guidance rather than a generic checklist.

01

What it helps cover

Doctor visits, outpatient services, preventive care and some medical equipment are common categories.

02

When enrollment matters

The initial window often surrounds a 65th birthday. Some people qualify for a special enrollment window.

03

Employer coverage

Active employment coverage, employer size, and the end of a job may affect decisions.

04

What it does not replace

Part B is not the same as drug coverage, extra private coverage, or custodial long-term care.

Five questions worth asking.

Tap a question for a little context. These are educational prompts, not personalized recommendations.

When is my applicable Part B enrollment window?

Many people have an initial enrollment period around age 65, but work status, disability and other circumstances can change eligibility and timing. Use Medicare.gov or Social Security to identify your exact window.

How does current employer coverage affect timing or coordination?

Coverage based on current employment and employer size can affect whether delaying Part B is appropriate. Retiree or COBRA coverage may follow different rules; verify before relying on an exception.

What services does Part B cover, and what costs might I pay?

Part B generally helps cover doctor and outpatient services and certain preventive care. Check current premiums, deductibles, coinsurance and covered services directly with Medicare rather than assuming all care is paid.

What happens if I delay enrollment and my job-based coverage ends?

Missing a qualifying enrollment period can result in a coverage gap or late-enrollment penalty. Confirm when employment or work-based coverage ends and which documents or special enrollment rules apply.

Which other coverage decisions are separate from Part B enrollment?

Prescription drug coverage, Medicare Advantage, supplemental coverage and long-term personal care are separate considerations. Review each only after clarifying Part B eligibility, timing and the rules that apply to you.
