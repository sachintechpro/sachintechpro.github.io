Bring these to a conversation

Five questions worth asking.

Tap a question for a little context. These are educational prompts, not personalized recommendations.

What would my household need immediately if I could no longer provide income?

Identify essential household expenses, debts, caregiving needs, and how long dependents might need help. That creates a clearer discussion than starting with a policy amount.

Which protections are already in place through work or privately?

Inventory employer benefits and personal protections, noting who is covered, any conditions, portability, and what happens when a job changes. Avoid assuming workplace coverage remains after employment.

How long could benefits last, and what could cause them to change or end?

Compare the duration of household obligations with the duration and renewal terms of any protection. Ask what circumstances could reduce, terminate, or change benefits.

How do costs, eligibility, exclusions, and future affordability compare?

Review eligibility, exclusions, premium changes, contractual guarantees and total cost over time. Ask for written terms and how affordability would be affected if income changed.

Who should receive any benefits and how would that align with our estate documents?

Review named beneficiaries after major life events and understand how funds for minors or dependents would be handled. An estate attorney can help coordinate any legal trust or guardianship arrangements.

Keep in mind

Actual eligibility, coverage, exclusions, costs, and claims are determined by written agreements and appropriately licensed professionals.

Don't leave out the essentials.

As life changes, revisit who can act for you and how your wishes will be carried out.
