Bring these to a conversation

Five questions worth asking.

Tap a question for a little context. These are educational prompts, not personalized recommendations.

How long could we manage essential expenses if income paused?

List essential monthly obligations and compare them with readily accessible savings, without entering numbers into this site. Consider what could be deferred and when cash might actually be available.

What support is available through work, savings, or existing coverage?

Review employer leave, sick time, applicable benefits, savings and any existing coverage. Check eligibility, benefit periods and coordination rather than assuming each source will pay in the same circumstances.

Which expenses might increase if health or caregiving needs changed?

An illness or caregiving responsibility may create travel, transport, childcare or home-support costs while reducing work income. Include these possibilities when estimating how much flexibility a household needs.

What waiting periods, exclusions, and claim requirements should I understand?

Read the terms for waiting periods, claim evidence, exclusions, maximum benefit periods and offsets with other payments. An appropriately licensed professional can explain the applicable contract and its limitations.

Who could manage bills and decisions if I needed help?

Identify a trusted person who knows how to locate essential bills and contacts. Ask an estate attorney whether a financial power of attorney or other legal authorization would be appropriate.

Keep in mind

Eligibility and benefits depend on employer policies, contracts, and applicable rules.

Don't leave out the essentials.

As life changes, revisit who can act for you and how your wishes will be carried out.
