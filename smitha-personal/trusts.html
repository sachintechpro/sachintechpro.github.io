Understand how a living trust fits

Understand how a living trust may help and why documents, titles, and beneficiaries still matter.

Explore the questions ↓
Four things to understand

Start with clarity.

Having a trust document and having a funded trust are different. California Courts notes that assets placed into a living trust may pass to beneficiaries without ordinary probate.

01

Who does what

A trustee manages trust property under the document; a successor trustee may step in when needed.

02

Funding matters

A trust usually needs appropriate assets titled or transferred into it to work as intended.

03

It is not every document

A trust does not replace all wills, health-care directives, powers of attorney, or beneficiary reviews.

04

Professional review

A California estate attorney can discuss costs, probate alternatives, tax questions, and special situations.

Bring these to a conversation

Five questions worth asking.

Tap a question for a little context. These are educational prompts, not personalized recommendations.

What problem are we solving with a trust in my circumstances?

Start with the outcome you need: continuity if you become incapacitated, clarity for beneficiaries, or possible probate planning. An attorney can explain whether a trust, another document, or a simpler arrangement fits your circumstances.

Which assets may be included, excluded, or handled through beneficiary designations?

Some property may pass by beneficiary designation or co-ownership rather than through a trust. Have an attorney review account and property types before changing titles or beneficiary forms.

How does trust funding work and how would it be verified?

Signing a trust does not automatically transfer assets into it. Ask the attorney for an asset-by-asset funding plan, who completes each transfer, and how deeds or account registrations will be confirmed.

Who should serve as trustee and what responsibilities come with that role?

A trustee manages assets under legal duties set out in the document. Choose a capable person or institution, name backups, and ask about recordkeeping, fees, potential conflicts, and successor responsibilities.

How might the trust need updating after marriage, a move, or a death?

Marriage, divorce, a move, death, new assets, and beneficiary changes can affect how the plan works. Ask an attorney when to review both the document and the funding or title of trust assets.

Keep in mind

A trust is not legally required for everyone. This is a planning conversation, not advice to buy or establish one.
