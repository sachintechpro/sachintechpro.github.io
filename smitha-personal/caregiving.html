CAREGIVER PATH · With Smitha

Support an aging loved one

Caring for a parent or older relative can involve family emotions, practical logistics, health needs, and money responsibilities. Thoughtful preparation makes it easier to respect their wishes.

Bring these to a conversation

Five questions worth asking.

Tap a question for a little context. These are educational prompts, not personalized recommendations.

What does my loved one want and what can they still manage independently?

Begin by asking your loved one what independence, privacy and support mean to them. Avoid assuming family members should take over tasks that the person can and wants to manage.

Who is legally authorized to make health and financial decisions?

An emergency contact does not automatically have authority to make medical or financial decisions. Review applicable directives, powers of attorney and consent with qualified legal or health professionals.

Which care services are covered and which would be paid privately?

Separate skilled medical services from ongoing personal care, transport or household assistance. Medicare generally does not cover most custodial long-term care; check coverage terms and official guidance.

How can we share responsibilities without overloading one caregiver?

List recurring responsibilities and identify a primary helper and a backup for each. Check each person's availability so one family member does not silently carry every task.

What records and emergency information should be accessible to the right people?

Keep a secure, consent-aware directory of care contacts, medicines, emergency preferences and legal documents. Share only what authorized helpers actually need.

Keep in mind

A family member’s wishes and legal rights come first. Do not upload their medical or financial records to this site.

Don't leave out the essentials.

As life changes, revisit who can act for you and how your wishes will be carried out.
