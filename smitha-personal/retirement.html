Five questions worth asking.

Tap a question for a little context. These are educational prompts, not personalized recommendations.

What income sources might be available and when could they begin?

List possible Social Security, pensions, workplace plans, savings and other income sources. Then note when each can begin and any eligibility or election decisions worth verifying.

How could inflation, longevity, or unexpected care affect the plan?

Consider how long you may need income, rising living costs and unexpected caregiving needs. Separate regular essentials from irregular costs so that one optimistic scenario does not drive the entire plan.

What is guaranteed, what can vary, and what limits access to money?

Identify which benefits are contractually guaranteed and which depend on markets or other assumptions. Ask about withdrawal restrictions, penalties, fees and access to emergency funds.

How might taxes and health expenses change across retirement stages?

Health coverage, taxable withdrawals and benefit timing can affect net resources. Medicare enrollment has its own rules, so check official guidance and ask qualified tax or financial professionals about your situation.

Which estate and beneficiary decisions should we revisit?

Review beneficiary forms, who could help with decisions, and how your estate documents align with current wishes. Changes in family, location or assets may justify an attorney review.

Keep in mind

This site does not calculate retirement income, recommend investments or guarantee payouts. Verify contracts, fees, rules and professional credentials.

Don't leave out the essentials.

As life changes, revisit who can act for you and how your wishes will be carried out.
