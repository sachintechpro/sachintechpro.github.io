The people around the plan

“Some of my friends say…”

Often, one person’s question is really about the people they love. Here are small, familiar moments that can help a family begin talking.

These are illustrative situations, not testimonials or stories about specific clients.

New parents · partners thinking about a child
“If something happened to us, my sister would know what to do.”

One parent may assume a relative already knows their wishes. The other may be wondering who would care for the child and how to make the parents’ intentions clear.

A gentle first question: What would we want a trusted person to know, and what should we ask an estate-planning attorney?
Adult child · aging parent
“Dad says I know what he wants, but we’ve never really talked about it.”

A daughter wants to help her father, while he wants to stay independent. The family may not know who can help with decisions if he cannot speak for himself.

A gentle first question: Which decisions matter most to Dad, and who should be part of discussing them?
Siblings · caring for a parent together
“We want to help Mom, but we don’t agree on what she would choose.”

One sibling is nearby and handles appointments; another helps with costs from a distance. Both want to respect their mother and avoid making assumptions for her.

A gentle first question: What does Mom want, and what support or professional guidance would help us honor it?
Blended family · spouse and children
“I want my partner to be okay, and I want my children to understand my wishes.”

A parent cares for a current spouse and children from an earlier relationship. Everyone may be unsure whether past choices still reflect the family today.

A gentle first question: Which wishes or documents may need an attorney’s review after our family changed?
Business owner · spouse and business partner
“My partner can keep the business moving, but what would my family need?”

A business owner relies on a co-owner for daily operations, while a spouse worries about household needs and who to contact if the owner is suddenly unavailable.

A gentle first question: What should my family and business partner each know, and which professionals should help us plan?
Couple · approaching retirement
“We’ve planned the next chapter, but we haven’t talked about what if one of us can’t manage things.”

One partner has always handled bills and paperwork. The other wants to feel prepared and know where important information and trusted contacts are kept.

A gentle first question: What would help both of us feel informed, included, and clear about our wishes?
A few clear answers

Questions families often ask

You do not need every answer before you begin. These basics can make the first conversation feel easier.

What does estate planning help a family talk through?

It can help you consider who you trust to make decisions, how to communicate your wishes, and how loved ones could be supported. The right documents and legal choices depend on your circumstances and state law, so an estate-planning attorney should advise on those decisions.

Does every family need a trust?

No. A trust is one possible planning tool; it is not right for every person or family. An attorney can explain what it may do, what it does not do, other options, costs, and the work needed to keep it current.

What happens in a 15-minute conversation?

It is a friendly introduction to hear what prompted you to reach out, identify the family members affected, and decide what questions may be useful to explore next. It is not legal advice, a full financial review, or a promise that a trust is appropriate.

Should my spouse, parent, or adult child join?

If the concern affects someone else, you can invite them if they are comfortable. You can also begin on your own and ask how to include them later. Each person’s preferences and privacy matter.

Do I need to bring documents or share financial details?

No. Start with the life change or question on your mind. This sample page does not send or store requests, so do not enter account numbers, identity documents, legal records, or other sensitive information here.

Who prepares legal documents and gives legal advice?

A qualified estate-planning attorney can explain your legal options and prepare the documents that fit your situation. Tax questions should go to a qualified tax professional. DeVI offers general educational prompts, not legal, tax, or financial advice.
